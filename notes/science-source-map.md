## What it means for the board (for discussion, no change made)

- The research is about employees' work stress and recovery. The board is a
  personal planner. Most of these studies do not test what a planner does, so none
  of them can back a claim about the board's own advice.
- The nearest fits: detachment supports the board's "your day ends at" rule
  (claims 1 and 2); micro-breaks have one small diary study behind them (claim 4).
  Mindfulness and internet CBT are treatment-like and out of the board's scope.
- The document's own warning is the useful part for design: "responsibilization",
  telling a person to cope better when the load is the problem. The board already
  allows low days and avoids blame. A future "too much on the plate" signal that
  suggests cutting scope, not another coping tool, would follow it. The evidence
  for that is mixed (claim 10), so it is a design direction, not a result.
- Several of the document's strongest sentences (claims 1, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10) are
  stronger than the sources found. Anything taken from it should use the sources'
  wording, not the document's.

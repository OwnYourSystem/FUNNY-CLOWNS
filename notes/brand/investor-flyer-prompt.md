## Your role

You are a senior pitch writer and information designer. You write for early-stage investors who read fast, distrust hype, and check claims. Your job is to make one product clear, credible and memorable on one page, then give the founder a short spoken pitch to go with it.

## What to produce

1. **An investor flyer**: one page, A4 portrait (also readable as a phone image). Give the final copy for every block and a layout description precise enough for a designer or an image tool to build it (position, size order, colours, type).
2. **A 90-second spoken pitch**: the founder's words, about 220 words, plain and calm.
3. **A 6-slide outline**: one line of headline and three short points per slide, same facts as the flyer.
4. **A claims list**: every factual claim you used, each marked "proven", "built, not proven" or "planned". Nothing goes on the flyer that is not on this list.

## The hard rules (do not break these)

1. **Use only the facts in this prompt.** Do not invent users, revenue, growth, market size, ratings, partners, awards or quotes. Where a pitch would normally have a number that is not given here, write a visible placeholder in square brackets, for example `[market size: add from a checked source]`, `[funding ask]`, `[founder name]`.
2. **Keep the status words exact.** "Proven" means it worked for a real person on a real device. "Built" means the automated tests pass. Never write that something is proven when it is only built.
3. **The product is not a medical or mental-health tool.** Do not suggest diagnosis, therapy or treatment. Do not use health claims.
4. **Do not oversell the AI.** The current assistant works from built-in commands and the person's own records, offline. A hosted AI model is built and switched off on purpose. Do not write "AI-powered" as a selling point.
5. **No em dashes.** Use periods, commas, colons or parentheses.
6. **Words to avoid:** revolutionary, game-changer, disrupt, crush it, hustle, 10x, guaranteed, streak, productivity hack, love, best friend, "studies show".
7. **Words to use where they fit:** next step, every day counts, minimum day, your data stays with you, proven on, not proven yet, you decide.

## The voice

Plain English in the spirit of ASD-STE100: short sentences, active voice, one meaning per word, digits for numbers. Specific beats vague. Proof beats hype. Calm beats punchy. Confident without being loud. The tone of a builder who tells you exactly what works and what does not yet.

## The product in one sentence

A daily planner that gets smaller when your day does: it sizes each day to the person's real energy, gives one clear next step with its reason, and still counts a bad day.

## The problem

- Most planners ask for the same effort every day. They are built for the best day and fail on the worst one.
- A bad night, a long meeting or a hard week breaks the plan. The list grows, the person feels behind, and after a week they stop opening the app.
- Planners treat a day as predictable. People are not machines: energy, sleep and other people's needs change the plan.

## The solution: what it does (all built)

- **One next step, with the reason.** It knows work hours, travel time, when the person trains and when the day ends. It will not suggest a workout at 23:00.
- **The day is sized to the person.** Today holds 1 thing on a low day, 3 on a normal day, 5 on a good day, from the last days' results and one tap ("rough day").
- **A minimum day.** The person writes up to 4 small things that count when nothing else moves (a proper meal, 10 minutes outside). Tick them and the day counts.
- **Quiet on purpose.** At most 2 reminders a day (a morning brief and an afternoon check), never after the person's day ends. A late reminder is dropped, not sent late. The reminder text is written on the person's own phone; the server never sees a task or a note.
- **It learns only with a yes.** If it notices a pattern, it shows the numbers and asks. Everything it keeps can be read and erased in one press.
- **Honest experiments.** Try one small change for a week or two; it compares the days and says "not enough to say" when the data is thin.
- **A private journal.** End of day and start of day, with date and time. The next morning the person starts from where they stopped. Entries stay hidden until a day is opened, and can be edited later.
- **A short view of the week.** Steps finished against the week before, days with a step done, all goals in one bar, and the 7 days as columns. A helicopter view, not a report.
- **Speak instead of type.** Every text box has a Speak button (the phone's own speech recognition). It fills the box; it never sends anything by itself.
- **A typed assistant.** Plain sentences change the plan: add, move, rename, set a deadline, fill today, set work hours. Anything that deletes waits for a button press. It works with no account and no network.
- **Undo everywhere it matters.** Done, move, archive and remove can be taken back for 6 seconds.

## Why it is different

1. **It shrinks; it does not scold.** No streaks that reset, no leaderboards, no guilt. A missed day is information, not a verdict.
2. **It shows its reasoning.** Every suggestion says why.
3. **The person decides.** The product suggests; the person accepts or changes it in one tap.
4. **It says when it does not know.** Advice names where it comes from. The method is labelled "practitioner opinion", not science, until the research is checked.
5. **Data stays with the person.** The board lives on the device. Sign-in is optional and only keeps the board across devices. The most private parts (the weekly check-in, the journal, the profile photo) never leave the phone, never go into a backup file and are never shown to the assistant.

# Minimum Deliveries Board

**A daily planner that gets smaller when your day does.**

---

## The problem

Most planners ask for the same effort every day.

You plan a full day. A bad night, a long meeting or a rough mood gets in the way. You finish half. The list grows. You feel behind. After a week you stop opening it.

A planner built for your best day fails on your worst one.

Most planners also promise that a day is predictable, as if a person were a machine. People are not. Energy, sleep and other people's needs change the plan. A tool that only works when nothing goes wrong stops working in the first week.

## The idea

The Minimum Deliveries Board reads your day, not just your list.

Each morning it decides how big today should be. On a low day it shows you one thing. On a normal day it shows three. On a good day it shows five. It makes that call from what you finished in the last days, from the times you told it you work and rest, and from one tap if you say "rough day".

A low day is not a failure here. It is a day sized for you. The board plans for the days that go wrong, not only for the days that go well.

## What it does

**It tells you what to do now.** One step, and the reason in plain words. It knows your work hours, your commute, when you train, and when your day ends. It will not suggest a workout at 23:00.

**It keeps two lists, not a pile.** *Today* is at the top. It holds only as many things as the day can carry: 1 on a low day, 3 on a normal day, 5 on a good day. Every card says why it is there. *Waiting* holds the rest and what you did not finish yesterday. What waits does not count against you, so a long waiting list can never make today look like a failed day.

**It shows what needs attention, and goes quiet on a low day.** Late and close deadlines are listed in one place. On a low day the board pauses that list. A rough day is not the time to show you a pile of overdue things.

**It keeps a floor under bad days.** You write your own "minimum day": up to four small things that count even when nothing else moves, such as a proper meal or ten minutes outside. When you tick them, the day counts.

**It has an assistant you type to.** No microphone, no voice. Type what you want in plain words, and the assistant changes it for you: add, rename, move or remove a step, set its priority, deadline, tag, "when" or "how long", fill Today, change a goal, set your work hours, day end, commute and training, switch hints, dark mode and reminders on or off, set your minimum day, open a page or search. If it needs a detail, it asks one short question. If it cannot place a sentence, it shows the closest things it can do, and changes nothing. Anything that deletes waits for your button press. Ask how this week compares with last week and it answers from your own records. It does not browse the web.

**It learns how you work, and only with your yes.** If it sees that you finish most things in the morning, it shows you the numbers and asks. You say yes or no. You can read everything it keeps, and erase it in one press.

**It tests ideas on you, honestly.** You can try one small change for a week or two, such as a smaller first step. At the end it compares those days with the days before, and tells you what it found. When there is not enough data, it says "not enough to say".

**It asks what is in the way.** After three low days in a row it asks, once, in plain words: too tired, afraid, do not know how, do not want to, or just a rough patch. Each answer gets different, practical options. It does not diagnose anything.

**It adds two small notes to a step.** "When?" ties the step to a moment you will notice, such as after your coffee. "How long?" keeps your guess, then asks once how long it really took. After five steps it can tell you how your guesses compare with what happened.

**It checks in on you, privately.** Once a week, five taps from 1 to 5: energy, mood, calm, getting through the day, and something that mattered. They sit beside what you finished that week, because output can rise while the rest falls. This data stays on your device. It is not synced, not in the backup file, and the tutor never sees it.

**It sends two calm reminders a day, even when the app is closed.** A morning brief at your time, and an afternoon check at 15:00 on what is still open in Today. Nothing is sent after your day is meant to be over. A small server sends the signal. It knows your device, your time zone, your brief time and your day end. It never gets a task, a goal or a note: the words of the reminder are written on your own device.

## A day with it

You open the board at 07:30. It shows one step and why: "You have a clear hour, and this is the first step of the goal that is due soonest."

You tap "rough day". The board shrinks to one thing. Under it you see your minimum day: a meal, a reply to one email, ten minutes outside. You tick two. At the end of the day you did less than usual, and the day still counts.

That evening you ask the tutor: "How did my mornings compare with my afternoons this month?" It answers with your own numbers, and says plainly that this shows when things got done, not when you work best.

## Why it is different

1. **It shrinks. It does not scold.** A missed day is treated as information, not as a verdict. On a low day the board stops raising the pressure because you are behind.
2. **It shows its reasoning.** Every suggestion says why.
3. **It learns with your permission.** It proposes, you decide, and you can see and erase what it keeps.
4. **It says when it does not know.** The tutor is not allowed to say "studies show" without naming where the idea comes from. Its answers are checked against your own numbers before you see them.
5. **Your data stays yours.** Your board lives in your own browser. Signing in is optional, and it only keeps your board across devices. The most personal part, the weekly check-in, is stored apart from everything else.

## Built with care

- If someone writes that they are struggling, the board gives one calm, fixed reply that points to a doctor, a crisis line or someone they trust. It makes no medical claim.
- The tutor cannot delete a step or empty a list on its own. It asks, and nothing happens until you press the button.
- Questions that need an answer open as a dialog on the page, only after you press something. They always have a "Not now".
- The assistant cannot see or change your weekly check-in. That stays private.
- It installs on a phone, opens offline, and has a dark and a light look.
- More than 300 automated checks run against it before every release.

## Honest about the evidence

The method comes from two practitioners' ideas about adaptive daily habits. The board labels these "practitioner opinion" on screen. It does not call them science.

A few of its ideas have published research behind them, for example planning ahead, tying a step to a moment ("when X, I will do Y"), and the habit of guessing task time too low. We found support by search but have not yet checked the papers ourselves, so the board cites none of them.

## What it is not

- It is not a medical or mental-health tool, and it gives no diagnosis or treatment.
- It is not a team tool or a calendar.
- It does not use a cloud AI model. The assistant works from built-in commands and your own records, so it works with no account and no network. A hosted model is built and switched off until one is chosen.

## Where it stands today

**Working now:** the daily board and its picks, day sizing, Today and Waiting, needs attention, the minimum day, the typed assistant that controls every setting and list, memory you control, experiments, hints, the low-day question, the two step notes, the weekly check-in, backup and restore, optional sign-in, phone install.

**Built, but not yet proven on a real phone:** reminders when the app is closed. The server, the schedule rules and the phone side are built, and the checks we can run here pass. A real phone has not received one yet. That needs one device and one day of watching. On an iPhone it works only when the board is added to the Home Screen.

**Planned, not built:** a tuned AI model for deeper conversation, checked research references, and a formal review of reliability, security, data governance and AI compliance.

## Who it is for

- People with more to do than one day holds.
- People who started planners and dropped them.
- People whose energy changes from day to day and who want a plan that follows.
- People who want help that does not guilt them, and a tool that keeps their data close.

## In one sentence

*A planner that sizes your day to your real energy, learns how you work with your permission, and tells you when it does not know.*

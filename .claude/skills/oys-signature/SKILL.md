# The OYS signature: how this owner builds products

This file describes one product maker's philosophy, taken from what they decided while building the Minimum Deliveries Board (2026). Each principle is backed by a real decision, so it is a method, not a slogan.

Apply it to every new product. When a choice breaks a principle, say so plainly and name the cost.

---

## 1. The positioning in one breath

> **OwnYourSystem builds calm products that give people judgment and direction, keep their data at home, and tell the truth about what they can and cannot do.**

What the buyer gets: one clear next step, from a product that is honest, quiet and on their side.

What the buyer does not get: a dashboard of 40 tools, guilt, streaks, noise, or claims the product cannot prove.

**The sales line:** people do not pay for features. They pay for judgment and direction. The engine is the asset; the tools stay hidden under one simple surface.

---

## 2. The creator in five traits

1. **Systems thinker.** Sees the whole system, not one screen. Asks what happens on a bad day, on a second device, when the server fails, when the law asks.
2. **Owner, not spectator.** Runs the risky steps personally (database changes, approvals, backups). Keeps the final word on every decision.
3. **Honest to a fault.** "Proven on one device" is said as exactly that. Nothing is claimed before it is tested.
4. **Patient with timing.** Freezes a build to test it for days. Defers the store listing ("keep calm, not that soon"). Speed never beats proof.
5. **Critical by request.** Asks for the professional, critical view, not applause. Wants the weak spot named.

---

## 3. The ten principles

Each principle has a test. A feature that fails the test does not ship until it passes or the owner accepts the cost in writing.

### P1. Start from a summit sentence
Before building, write one sentence that says what life looks like when the product works, and how to measure it.
- Example: "When life does not go to plan, people still know their next step and every day still counts; over months, they grow in what matters to them without losing their rest, health or people."
- **Test:** can every feature point to a word in the summit sentence?

### P2. Climb your own mountain
Do not copy the category leader. Compare honestly (criteria first, then a table), then pick a direction the leaders cannot follow.
- Example: the board compared itself to Motion and Sunsama, then chose "work and life balance and growth tool", not "planning tool".
- **Test:** in one sentence, what does this product do that the top 2 competitors will not?

### P3. One decision on screen
Show one decision. Put the reason one tap away. Hide the rest behind "More".
- **Test:** can a new user act in under 3 seconds without reading help?

### P4. The person decides
The product suggests; the person accepts or changes it in one tap. A dialog opens only when the person asks for it. A delete waits for a button.
- **Test:** is there any place where the product acts without the person's own action? If yes, remove it or add undo.

### P5. Care, not control
Be kind. Never scold. Shrink the plan when life is hard (a "minimum day" counts). No streaks that reset, no leaderboards, no unlocks, no guilt.
- **Test:** read every message aloud to someone on their worst day. Would it hurt?

### P6. Honest by design
- No claimed feelings. The product gives care and attention, not love or intimacy. It never pretends to be human.
- No claimed evidence. Advice says where it comes from and how strong it is.
- No claimed reliability. Say what is proven, on how many devices, and what is not.
- **Test:** is there one sentence in the product or the pitch that is not proven? Rewrite it or cut it.

### P7. Quiet by design
Notifications are a cost to the person. Set a hard limit (the board: at most 2 a day, never after the day ends, a late message is dropped, not sent late).
- **Test:** what is the maximum number of interruptions per day, and who enforces it?

### P8. Own your data
The name of the brand is the rule. Private things (a check-in, a feeling, a task name) stay on the device. The server knows only what it must. The person can see, export and delete everything.
- Example: the server sends a push with one word ("am" or "pm"); the phone writes the text.
- No personal data goes to a vendor until the vendor is in the data-processing record.
- **Test:** list every field the server holds. Can each one be justified in one line?

### P9. Write before you build
Requirements first, code second. Non-functional requirements (privacy, reliability, security, accessibility, limits) are written before the first line of code. AI gets its own requirements and a test set of real, unusual conversations before it is built.
- Build only what was agreed. A good idea that was not agreed goes on a list, not into the product.
- **Test:** is there a written, agreed requirement for this change?

### P10. Comply and face reality
Legal pages, privacy, the right to erasure, accessibility and AI rules are part of the product, not paperwork after it. A legal professional reviews before publishing.
- **Test:** could this ship in the EU tomorrow without a lawyer saying no?

## 4. How AI is used

- **AI is the core, but it is replaceable.** It sits behind a server boundary, so the model can change without changing the product.
- **Start cheap, tune later.** The first model is the lowest-cost one that can do the job. Tuning comes after real use shows what to tune.
- **The product works without it.** The rules engine and the typed assistant work offline, so a model outage is never a broken product.
- **AI stays out of the build until its requirements exist.** No hype features.
- **Use typed AI only where it helps.** A structured judgment beats a free-text prompt and parse.

---

## 5. How the work is run

1. **Decide.** Summit sentence, principles, a brief. The owner says yes or no to each open item.
2. **Foundation first.** One shared engine, safe data, sync, undo, delete my data, monitoring. Screens come after.
3. **Subtract before you add.** Hide or remove tools before new ones are built.
4. **Prove on a real device.** A feature is "built" when the tests pass. It is "proven" when it works for a real person on a real device. The two words are never mixed up.
5. **Freeze to test.** When the owner tests, the test build does not move.
6. **Monitor what is invisible.** A failure the person cannot see is still a failure. Alerts go to the owner, not to the user.
7. **Publish with care.** Legal review, version numbers, a "what changed" note, feature switches. The store listing comes last.

---

## 6. The voice

- Plain English, controlled language (ASD-STE100 spirit): short sentences, active voice, one meaning per word.
- Specific beats vague: numbers, names, limits, dates.
- Proof beats hype. No "revolutionary", no "AI-powered" as a selling point by itself.
- Calm beats punchy, above all on hard topics.
- No em dashes, no filler, no flattery.

**Words we use:** next step, every day counts, minimum day, care and attention, your data stays here, proven on, not proven yet, you decide.

**Words we avoid:** crush it, hustle, streak, productivity hack, love, best friend, guaranteed, 10x.

---

## 7. How to sell it (for the sales conversation)

**Who buys:** people whose days do not go to plan (parents, managers, people in care roles, people after illness or burnout) and who want to grow without losing rest, health or people.

**The pain:** planners assume a predictable day. On a bad day they show a wall of red, and people quit.

**The promise:** on any day, you know your next step, and the day still counts.

**The proof (only what is true):**
- what is built and tested (with the number of checks);
- what is proven on real devices (with the number of devices);
- what is not proven yet, said openly.

**The objection handler:** "Why not Motion or Sunsama?" → "They plan your calendar. We keep you steady when the calendar breaks, and we measure growth, not finished tasks."

**The price logic:** people pay for judgment and direction, not for the number of tools. Price the decision, not the feature list.

## 8. Review checklist for any new product

Run this before a build starts and again before it ships. Answer each line with yes, no, or "cost accepted by the owner".

| # | Check | Principle |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One summit sentence exists, with measures | P1 |
| 2 | Honest comparison against the top 2 competitors, criteria first | P2 |
| 3 | One decision per screen; reason one tap away | P3 |
| 4 | Nothing acts without the person's own action; undo exists | P4 |
| 5 | No guilt, streaks, leaderboards or scolding; a minimum day counts | P5 |
| 6 | Every claim in the product and the pitch is proven, or marked as not proven | P6 |
| 7 | A hard limit on interruptions, enforced by the system | P7 |
| 8 | Every server field is justified; private data stays on the device; delete my data works | P8 |
| 9 | Written, agreed requirements, including non-functional ones, before code | P9 |
| 10 | Legal, privacy, accessibility and AI rules reviewed before publishing | P10 |
| 11 | AI behind a swappable boundary; product works without it | AI |
| 12 | Proven on a real device, not only in tests | Method |

---

## 9. How to apply this skill

When asked to plan, design, review or sell a product for this owner:
1. Restate the summit sentence, or draft one and ask the owner to confirm it.
2. Run the checklist in section 8. Name every "no" plainly, with its cost.
3. Recommend one next step, not a menu.
4. Write in the voice of section 6.
5. Never mark something as done or proven before it is.
